## Purpose and terminology

This document analyzes why a mission or piece of equipment stopped operating, lost communication, failed to land, or reached the end of its planned service. Do not label every inactive object a “failed mission.”

Use one of these outcome categories:
- **Planned completion:** primary mission objectives or planned operations concluded.
- **Extended mission ended:** operations continued beyond the original plan and later ended.
- **Communication loss:** contact ceased; the physical cause may or may not be known.
- **Confirmed technical failure:** a documented hardware, software, or systems failure.
- **Environmental constraint:** dust, temperature, terrain, radiation, or another environmental factor affected operations.
- **Landing failure:** the spacecraft did not complete a successful landing.
- **Deliberate decommissioning / disposal:** the agency intentionally ended operations.
- **Undetermined:** available evidence does not establish the cause.

## Starter analysis: Opportunity

**Event:** Opportunity stopped communicating during the 2018 planet-encircling dust event.

Research points:
- Establish the dates of the dust event and last communication from NASA records.
- Explain how reduced sunlight affected the rover's solar energy generation.
- Identify the power and thermal conditions reported by NASA.
- Summarize the recovery commands and listening campaigns.
- Record NASA's February 2019 announcement that the mission was complete.

Important distinction: the dust event and loss of communication are documented. Do not claim to know the rover's exact final physical condition unless a source establishes it.

## Starter analysis: Spirit

**Event:** Spirit became embedded in soft soil and later stopped communicating.

Research points:
- Document the rover's mobility problem and attempts to free it.
- Explain why its position affected winter energy and thermal survival.
- Record the last communication date and NASA's recovery efforts.
- Distinguish the end of mobility from the later end of communications and the official mission closure.

## Starter analysis: Mars Pathfinder / Sojourner

**Event:** Communications with the Pathfinder lander ceased in September 1997.

Research points:
- Establish the final communication sequence from NASA records.
- Explain the lander’s role as the rover's communications relay.
- Investigate reported system and battery/thermal issues without presenting unconfirmed explanations as fact.
- Distinguish Sojourner's rover operations from the overall Pathfinder lander mission.

## Analysis standards

1. Use primary agency statements for official status and dates.
2. Use engineering papers or technical reports for root-cause analysis.
3. Separate symptoms, cause, contributing factors, and outcome.
4. Use confidence labels: `Confirmed`, `Probable`, `Possible`, `Unknown`.
5. Never infer a failure cause solely because a mission stopped communicating.
