## Purpose and scope

This document records the historical background of selected robotic and crewed missions that left human-made equipment on or near the Moon and Mars. “Abandoned” is not used as a blanket label: equipment may be inactive, intentionally discarded, stranded, crashed, or part of a mission that ended normally. Each record must use the most accurate status supported by evidence.

#### 4. Design and instruments
Describe the equipment's main systems and scientific instruments. Include instrument names only when confirmed by an official technical source.

#### 5. Launch and journey
Record the launch vehicle, launch date, cruise phase, major trajectory events, and arrival date. Do not infer missing details.

#### 6. Landing and deployment
Explain the landing or deployment method, landing date, site, and evidence for the reported location.

#### 7. Operations and major events
Provide a chronological account of major activities, discoveries, anomalies, and mission milestones. Use dates and cite each significant claim.

#### 8. Mission conclusion
Explain how operations ended. Classify the outcome as one of:
- Planned completion
- Extended mission ended
- Communication lost
- Hardware / software failure
- Landing failure
- Deliberately decommissioned
- Unknown / not established

A mission ending is not automatically a “failure.”

#### 9. Historical and scientific significance
Explain what the mission contributed to exploration, engineering, or planetary science.

#### 10. Researcher's analysis
Write an original, evidence-based interpretation. Separate documented facts from interpretation and identify uncertainty.

#### 11. Sources
List full references and indicate which claims each source supports.

## Starter case study: Opportunity rover

### Mission identification
- Equipment: Opportunity (Mars Exploration Rover-B / MER-B)
- Agency: NASA
- Celestial body: Mars
- Equipment type: Solar-powered mobile rover
- Landing site: Meridiani Planum
- Landing date: 2004-01-25 (UTC date commonly reported; verify exact event convention)
- Mission conclusion: NASA declared the mission complete in February 2019 after unsuccessful attempts to re-establish communication following a major 2018 dust storm.

### Historical background and objectives
Opportunity was one of NASA's twin Mars Exploration Rovers, alongside Spirit. The mission investigated rocks and soils to understand the history of water and past environmental conditions on Mars. Its work at Meridiani Planum provided evidence of ancient aqueous processes.

### Operations and discoveries
Research and document the rover's traverse, key rock outcrops, hematite-rich spherules, and evidence interpreted as indicating past interaction with liquid water. Identify the instruments and exact findings from NASA mission pages and peer-reviewed publications rather than relying on unsourced summaries.

### Mission conclusion
A planet-encircling dust event in 2018 reduced sunlight reaching the rover's solar panels. Opportunity stopped communicating in June 2018. NASA continued recovery attempts and announced the end of the mission in February 2019. Avoid claiming that the dust storm physically destroyed the rover unless a source establishes that; distinguish the observed loss of power/communication from the inferred final hardware condition.

- Add verified instrument names and major dated milestones.
- Cite primary NASA records and relevant scientific papers.
- Record the rover's landing coordinates with the coordinate system and source.

## Starter case study: Spirit rover

### Mission identification
- Equipment: Spirit (Mars Exploration Rover-A / MER-A)
- Agency: NASA
- Celestial body: Mars
- Equipment type: Solar-powered mobile rover
- Landing site: Gusev Crater
- Landing date: 2004-01-04 (UTC date commonly reported; verify exact event convention)
- Mission conclusion: NASA ended recovery efforts in 2011 after the rover stopped communicating in March 2010.

### Historical background and objectives
Spirit and Opportunity were designed to investigate Martian geology and assess evidence of past water. Spirit explored Gusev Crater and made observations of rocks, soils, and volcanic or hydrothermal environments.

### Operations and discoveries
Document Spirit's traverse, scientific instruments, major discoveries, and the evidence supporting interpretations of past water-related processes. Identify which findings are confirmed observations and which are scientific interpretations.

### Mission conclusion
Spirit became embedded in soft soil in 2009 and could no longer move to improve its position for winter. It continued operating as a stationary science platform before communication ceased in March 2010. NASA announced the end of the mission in May 2011. Verify the exact dates and engineering explanation from primary sources.

- Document the final communications and recovery attempts.
- Identify key scientific findings with primary references.
- Confirm landing coordinates and coordinate frame.

## Starter case study: Sojourner rover / Mars Pathfinder

### Mission identification
- Equipment: Sojourner
- Mission: Mars Pathfinder
- Agency: NASA
- Celestial body: Mars
- Equipment type: Small robotic rover
- Landing site: Ares Vallis
- Landing date: 1997-07-04 (UTC date commonly reported)
- Mission conclusion: The lander/rover mission ended after communication with the lander was lost in September 1997.

### Historical background and objectives
Mars Pathfinder demonstrated a comparatively low-cost approach to Mars landing and surface exploration. Sojourner was the first successful Mars rover and examined nearby rocks and soil while the lander served as a communications relay.

### Operations and discoveries
Research Sojourner's mobility, camera and instrument package, rock/soil observations, and the role of the Pathfinder lander. Explain the distinction between the rover's operational period and the overall lander mission.

### Mission conclusion
The Pathfinder lander stopped communicating in September 1997. Research the documented sequence of communications problems and NASA's final status announcement. Do not attribute the loss to a single cause unless primary sources support it.

- Confirm final communication date and mission closure date.
- Add instrument details and selected scientific findings.
- Verify the landing coordinates and coordinate convention.
- Cite NASA mission and archival records.

## Writing and evidence standards

1. Use original wording; do not copy paragraphs from websites.
2. Cite a source immediately after each factual paragraph or in numbered footnotes.
3. Prefer NASA, ESA, JAXA, national space agency archives, peer-reviewed papers, and mission data archives.
4. Record exact dates and time zones where relevant.
5. Mark uncertain claims as `Unconfirmed` or `Requires verification`.
6. Distinguish equipment status from mission status.
7. Record the date each source was accessed.
8. Keep a research log and Git commit history for traceability.
